\section{Rigidity of exact gradient merge}\label{sec:rigidity}

For a target ordered set \([r]\), consider deterministic triangular weights
\(W_{ij}\) and the connection-plus-mean potential
\[
F_i(b)=\sum_{j>i}\log\Phi(-b_j)
-\frac1d\sum_{i<j<q}W_{jq}m(b_j)m(b_q).
\tag{3.1}
\]
At the central cutoff,
\[
 \partial_jF_i(c\mathbf1)
 =-m_0-\frac{m_0m'_0}{d}
 \sum_{\substack{q>i\\q\ne j}}W_{jq}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Unique exact-merge array]\label{prop:rigidity}
If the linear term in (3.2), multiplied by
\(b_j-c=\sqrt d\,h_{ij}\), must equal \(-W_{ij}h_{ij}\) at every boundary,
then the weights are uniquely determined by
\[
 W_{ij}=m_0\sqrt d+
 \frac{m_0m'_0}{\sqrt d}
 \sum_{\substack{q>i\\q\ne j}}W_{jq}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Uniform or edge-dependent potential rescaling creates no additional effective
degree of freedom.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient comparison gives (3.3).  Process the first endpoint in
decreasing order.  Every weight on the right side has already been defined,
so the system is triangular and has a unique solution.  If the potential is
scaled by \(\kappa\), set \(W=\kappa T\); the same coefficient comparison
again yields (3.3) for \(W\).  Edge-dependent scalar factors likewise only
rename the effective coefficients.
\end{proof}

The feedback operator in (3.3) has row norm at most
\(\rho=m_0m'_0/D=o(1)\).  Its accumulated increment is
\[
 2A_0\binom r4[1+O(\rho)].
\tag{3.4}
\]
With the source centered coefficient \(m_0^2[1+o(1)]\), the exact-merge
ledger contributes the previously reviewed term
\((1+o(1))/(96\log C)\).  Exact feedback evaluation can change only the
next order.
