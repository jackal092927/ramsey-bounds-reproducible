\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:unified-introduction}

For positive integers $k,\ell$, the Ramsey number $R(k,\ell)$ is the
least $n$ such that every red--blue colouring of $K_n$ contains a red
$K_k$ or a blue $K_\ell$.  The classical upper and lower exponential
scales originate in the work of Erd\H{o}s--Szekeres and Erd\H{o}s
\cite{ErdosSzekeres1935,Erdos1947}.  Modern improvements combine delicate
analytic inequalities, asymptotic reductions, and computer-assisted
certificates.  In the finite regime, SAT solvers can explore highly
constrained graph spaces, but a solver outcome becomes a theorem only after
the graph-to-CNF reduction and a proof trace have both been checked.

This article places four results developed here in one manuscript.  They concern
different regimes and use different mathematics; their unity is a common
claim-to-evidence discipline rather than a claimed common proof mechanism.
The first two give conditional, source-relative refinements of asymptotic
ledgers, the third establishes a local finite obstruction without improving a
tabulated Ramsey number, and the fourth gives a quantum query algorithm for
constructive Ramsey search without changing an extremal bound.

\subsection{Diagonal upper bound}

Part~I combines a certified six-stage off-diagonal rate, an exact-diagonal
correlation inequality, and the version-pinned regularization,
parameterized-book, and spine-compatibility interfaces of Yang and Mao
\cite{YangMao2026}.  The positive-moment and tail transfer is proved locally.
Under the source interfaces stated in
\cref{up:ass:yang-mao} and the certified rate theorem
\cref{up:thm:p6-rate}, it proves
\[
 R(k,k)\le (3.780685290)^{k+o(k)},
\]
with certified unrounded base less than
$3.780685288379640114$.  This is an asymptotic, source-relative,
computer-assisted theorem.  It supplies neither an effective finite-$k$
threshold nor a global optimum or priority claim.

\subsection{Fixed-ratio lower bound}

Part~II assumes the version-pinned items (S1), (S2), (S4), and (S5) imported
from the Gaussian construction of Hunter, Milojevi\'c and Sudakov and the
exact cumulant input of Lin and Niu \cite{HMS2025,LinNiu2026}.  The weighted
reverse-propagation rule (S3) is proved locally from the indicator kernel,
projection identities, and the tower property.  After fixing the absolute
source constant $K$, those four source items give an existential threshold
$C_0=C_0(K)>1$ such that, for each fixed $C\ge C_0$,
\[
 \liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\frac1\ell
 \log R(\ell,\lfloor C\ell\rfloor)
 \ge -\frac12\log p_C+\frac{B_R(C)}2+G_*(C)+\widehat H_*(C),
\]
where
\[
 \widehat H_*(C)=\frac{1+o_{C\to\infty}(1)}{64\log C}>0.
\]
The order of limits is part of the statement and proof: after fixing $K$ and
$C$, one takes $\ell\to\infty$, then sends the auxiliary cutoff
$w\downarrow\omega_0$, and only afterwards studies the leading behaviour as
$C\to\infty$.  The threshold $C_0(K)$ is non-effective, and the theorem does
not assert a strict comparison with every published final constant under a
different normalization.

\subsection{Finite proof-carrying barrier}

Part~III starts from the public $99$-vertex construction of Nagda, Raghavan,
and Thakurta~\cite{NagdaRaghavanThakurta2026} and fixes a labelled,
content-addressed one-vertex extension $H$ on $100$ vertices with $827$
edges, no independent set of order $18$, and one triangle.  If
additions outside $E(H)$ are free and only deletions from $E(H)$ are
charged, semantic reconstruction and six DRAT refutations prove that every
graph $F$ on the same labelled vertex set that is triangle-free and satisfies
$\alpha(F)<18$ obeys $|E(H)\setminus E(F)|\ge7$.  Exact seven remains
\textsc{unknown}.  Within branch 1, four additional proof-checked singleton
consequences force four named seed edges to be preserved.  Together with a
degree cap they exclude $87.8832136551\ldots\%$ of raw residual supports.  A
checked satisfying model proves that the common relaxation is nonempty, but
the model contains an independent 18-set and is not a repair.  The theorem is
strengthened methodologically by a maximal-triangle-free existence normal form
and a complete four-bank mask union.  Their combined, byte-audited branch-1
formula has (639290) variables and (1972360) clauses; its one bounded call
returned only \textsc{unknown}.  The theorem is
local to this seed and metric: it neither constructs a $100$-vertex
$(3,18)$-Ramsey graph nor implies $R(3,18)\ge101$.

\subsection{Quantum constructive search}

Part~IV quantizes the elementary majority-neighbourhood recursion.  For a
graph on at least $4^{K-1}$ vertices, capped uniform search on implicit
candidate predicates, conditional concentration, and a scale-aware error
schedule find a $K$-clique or $K$-vertex independent set using
\[
 O\!\left(2^K K\log\frac K\eta\right)
\]
coherent edge queries with failure probability at most $\eta$.  At
$N=2^n$ this finds order $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ using
$O(\sqrt N\log N\log(\log N/\eta))$ queries.  A random-Painter/Yao argument
gives a classical randomized lower bound
$\Omega(2^{(2-\sqrt2)K})$, still below the quantum upper bound.  Accordingly,
we claim a near-quadratic improvement over the standard explicit recursion,
not a separation from every classical algorithm.

A second, estimation-free algorithm samples one uniform candidate and follows
its edge colour, thereby choosing a branch in proportion to its size.  Its
survival induction gives
$O(2^K K^2\log K\log(1/\eta))$ queries and extends to every number of edge
colours.  Although its two-colour polynomial factor is weaker than the
scale-aware theorem, it isolates the simplest mechanism and shows that the
method is not binary-specific.

The exact-model prior-art audit finds an unresolved incompatibility with the
$N^{1-o(1)}$ quantum lower bound printed by Jain, Li, Robere, and Xun:
substitution into their Ramsey reduction and the cited multi-collision theorem
gives exponent at most $1/24$.  The manuscript records the calculation but
does not present it as an author-confirmed correction.

\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:evidence-taxonomy} states the evidence taxonomy and trust
boundaries used throughout.  Parts~I--IV give the complete arguments and
their result-specific verification records.  The unified reproduction
contract appears in \cref{sec:unified-reproducibility}; full technical
records, proof ledgers, and artifact tables are retained in the appendices.
Symbols introduced inside a Part are local to that Part unless explicitly
declared otherwise.  This convention prevents unrelated analytic functions,
Gaussian potentials, and finite graphs from being conflated.
