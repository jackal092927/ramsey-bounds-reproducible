\section{Prior work and comparison with a reported lower bound}
\label{sec:prior-art}

Previous quantum work under the phrase ``Ramsey algorithm'' has studied the
existence of avoidance colourings through adiabatic Hamiltonians and
small-instance experiments \cite{GaitanClark2012,BianEtAl2013}, exhaustive
search over the $2^{\binom V2}$ labelled graphs defining a Ramsey threshold
\cite{Wang2016}, quantum counting for hypergraph Ramsey numbers
\cite{QuLiWangBaoCao2013}, and bound discovery through adiabatic optimization
or QUBO reductions \cite{RanjbarMacreadyClarkGaitan2016,PionMniszewski2025}.
Their square-root improvements concern spaces of graph colourings rather than
the vertex set of one input graph.  Closer oracle-model results include
clique and independent-set query algorithms \cite{Doern2005}, quantum
subset-finding \cite{ChildsEisenberg2005}, learning-graph algorithms for fixed
subgraph containment \cite{LeeMagniezSantha2012,Zhu2012}, and quantum
backtracking \cite{Montanaro2018}.  Our approach instead uses the Ramsey
totality promise together with an implicit-set survival invariant.  To our
knowledge, no earlier work uses implicit-set quantum sampling to implement the
constructive Erd\H{o}s--Szekeres proof; this priority statement concerns the
exact oracle model and sources compared here.

Ramsey search has also been connected to weak-pigeonhole proof complexity and
to structured-pigeonhole search for circuit-encoded homogeneous sets
\cite{Krajicek2001,Krajicek2005}.  It lies in the polynomial long-choice class
PLC, which captures iterated pigeonhole arguments
\cite{PasarkarPapadimitriouYannakakis2023}.

\paragraph{Comparison at the formal default parameters.}
The closest exact-model treatment \cite{JainLiRobereXun2024} uses
$N=2^n$ vertices in Definition II.6 and sets the default target to $K=n/2$
after Definition II.7.  We use these formal definitions throughout this
comparison.  The unnumbered ``Query complexity of RAMSEY'' paragraph in
Section III (p.~415) reports an $N^{1-o(1)}$ quantum lower bound via
Lemma III.1 and the Liu--Zhandry multicollision theorem
\cite{LiuZhandry2019}.  The graph-hash product in Lemma III.1 can be written
off the diagonal as
$A_h(u,v)=\mathbf1[h(u)=h(v)]\lor A_0(h(u),h(v))$, with
$A_h(u,u)=0$.  It is a valid symmetric graph and has an $O(1)$-query coherent
implementation from the value oracle for $h$, using computation and
uncomputation of its two values.  Thus the valid-graph promise admits this
reduction.  Corollary~\ref{cor:unpromised} also aligns our upper bound with the
locally verifiable arbitrary-circuit relation.  Moreover,
\cref{cor:succinct} returns $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ vertices, so discarding one
if necessary solves the default target up to the suppressed rounding
convention.  The oracle model and target convention therefore yield the same
parameter regime for the two statements.

\paragraph{Direct parameter substitution.}
Write $H$ for the multicollision range size and $G$ for the number of
vertices in the Ramsey instance.  For fixed multiplicity $t$, the sufficient
parameter condition in \cite[Theorem I.3]{JainLiRobereXun2024} is
$G\ge H^{4t}/4^t$.  At the boundary
$G=\Theta_t(H^{4t})$, Liu--Zhandry's exponent in $H$ is
$\alpha_t=(2^{t-1}-1)/(2^t-1)$.  The inherited exponent is therefore
\[
 \frac{\alpha_t}{4t}
 =\frac{2^{t-1}-1}{4t(2^t-1)}\le\frac1{24},
 \qquad t\ge2,
\]
with equality at $t=2$.  Indeed, $t=2$ gives $1/24$, while for $t\ge3$
the strict inequality $\alpha_t<1/2$ gives
$\alpha_t/(4t)<1/(8t)\le1/24$.  Increasing $G$ relative to $H$ cannot
improve this transferred exponent.  This substitution remains within the fixed-multiplicity regime of the
cited Liu--Zhandry result.  This
calculation isolates the exponent obtained from the two cited statements
under the formal default parameters.

The two stated ingredients therefore transfer an exponent no larger than
$1/24$.  Obtaining the reported $N^{1-o(1)}$ exponent in the same
bounded-error quantum-query relation requires an additional ingredient or a
different normalization.  This comparison is confined to the unnumbered
query-complexity discussion in Section III of
\cite{JainLiRobereXun2024}.
