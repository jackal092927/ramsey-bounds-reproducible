\section{Discussion and open questions}
\label{sec:scope}

The query improvement rests on two complementary ideas.  First, the algorithm
stores each candidate set as the conjunction of the adjacency constraints
created by earlier pivots.  This representation supports quantum sampling
without enumerating the surviving vertices.  Second, the scale-aware analysis
allocates estimation error according to the cost of the recursion level.  The
resulting schedule keeps the cumulative loss in candidate-set size bounded
while concentrating most of the sampling effort at the less expensive early
levels.  \Cref{tab:bounds} summarizes the resulting landscape; we close with
the questions it leaves open.

\paragraph{Randomized worst case.}
The randomized query complexity of the Ramsey relation on $N=2^n$ vertices
lies between $\Omega(N^{1-1/\sqrt2})$ (\cref{prop:classical-lower}) and the
$O(N)$ of the explicit recursion.  We do not know a randomized algorithm
with $o(N)$ worst-case queries: classical rejection sampling from the
implicit set at depth $i$ costs $2^i$ membership tests, so the deep levels
of the recursion cost $\Theta(N)$ classically, whereas amplitude
amplification makes them cost $\Theta(\sqrt N)$.  A randomized lower bound
of $N^{1/2+\Omega(1)}$ would separate the worst-case quantum and randomized
complexities.

\paragraph{Quantum lower bounds.}
By \cref{prop:quantum-lower,cor:succinct}, the quantum query complexity
lies between $\Omega(N^{1/12})$ and $O(\sqrt N\log N\log\log N)$.  Is
$\sqrt N$ optimal?  The final levels of the recursion search sets of
constant size inside a universe of size $\Theta(N)$, which costs
$\Theta(\sqrt N)$ for unstructured search, but these sets are defined by
$O(\log N)$ adjacency constraints, and the optimality of Grover search does
not apply to them.

\paragraph{Random graphs.}
\Cref{cor:separation} separates the quantum and randomized complexities on
$G(N,1/2)$, but no nontrivial quantum lower bound specific to that
distribution is known, so the quantum exponent $1/4$ may not be optimal.  On the classical side, Conlon et
al.\ \cite{ConlonFoxGrinshpunHe2019} conjecture that the exponent
$2-\sqrt2$ in the lower bound $2^{(2-\sqrt2)K}$ is not tight for cliques of
fixed order and that the truth is $2^{(2/3+o(1))K}$, which would correspond
to $N^{1/3+o(1)}$ at the Ramsey scale; their branch-and-fill strategy may
also admit a quantum version with a smaller exponent than $1/4$.

\paragraph{Extensions.}
The implicit-set method may extend to off-diagonal and hypergraph Ramsey
search.  Quantum walks give sublinear-query algorithms for fixed
sub-hypergraph containment \cite{LeGallNishimuraTani2016}; a Ramsey
algorithm would additionally need to exploit the totality and recursive
structure of the problem.  For multiple edge colours, \cref{cor:multicolour}
gives a size-biased algorithm, while a scale-aware analogue may yield a
sharper dependence on the recursion depth.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
Appendix~\ref{app:experiments} reports finite experiments illustrating the
survival recurrence and the conditional uniformity of the sampler.  They are
sanity checks; the query bounds follow from the analytic proofs in
\cref{sec:model,sec:algorithm,sec:correctness,sec:complexity} and the
appendices.  The paper claims query bounds only: it makes no claim about
gate complexity, hardware, or a worst-case separation from every
randomized algorithm.
