\section{Evidence boundary, open problems, and conclusion}
\label{sec:scope}

The audit in \cref{app:reproducibility} checks the recurrence, error schedule,
split-tree bound, exponent translation, and small sampler instances.  These
are falsification checks, not speedup evidence; the theorem is the analytic
argument in \cref{sec:model,sec:algorithm,sec:correctness,sec:complexity}.

\paragraph{Small proof-of-concept experiments.}
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} reports 5,000 seeded runs at $K=3,N=16$,
an explicit 16-state Grover sampler check, and exact small split-tree programs.
They check sampler symmetry and end-to-end combinatorial logic, but are not a
full coherent simulation or evidence of wall-clock or hardware speedup.

\paragraph{AI-assisted research process.}
OpenAI Codex assisted brainstorming, proof and query-analysis checks,
literature comparison, executable checks, and editing; ChatGPT Pro assisted
brainstorming and adversarial review.  Outputs were treated as untrusted:
the claims rest on the proofs, primary sources, and reproducible checks, and
the human authors retain full responsibility.

Three open problems delimit the contribution.  First, clarify whether an
alternative normalization or derivation resolves the apparent parameter
mismatch in the Jain--Li--Robere--Xun quantum lower-bound remark.  Second,
determine
whether a randomized classical algorithm can match the
$\widetilde O(\sqrt M)$ upper bound; \cref{prop:classical-lower} leaves a
large exponent gap.  Third, determine whether the implicit-set method can be
adapted to off-diagonal or hypergraph Ramsey total search, and whether the
multicolour bound in \cref{cor:multicolour} can be sharpened by a scale-aware
estimator.

In summary, the constructive Ramsey recursion can be implemented without
materializing its exponentially shrinking candidate sets.  Capped uniform
search and a scale-aware error schedule give a
$\widetilde O(\sqrt N)$ query algorithm at the succinct Ramsey scale.  This
does not imply a new numerical Ramsey bound, a physical speedup, or a
quantum-versus-all-classical separation.
