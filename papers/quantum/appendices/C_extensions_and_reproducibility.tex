\section{Multicolour extension and executable checks}

\subsection{A multicolour extension of size-biased survival}
\label{app:multicolour}

The size-biased mechanism is not specific to two colours.  Suppose every edge
has one of $q\ge2$ colours, and let
\[
 r=q(K-2)+1,
 \qquad
 B_d=1+q+\cdots+q^{d-1}=\frac{q^d-1}{q-1}.
\]
Choose the power of two
\[
 L_q=2^{\lceil\log_2(2B_r)\rceil},
\]
so $2B_r\le L_q<4B_r$.  If the $s-1$ non-pivot vertices split into sizes
$a_1,\ldots,a_q$, the induction in \cref{lem:size-biased-survival} becomes
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^q (a_j-B_{d-1})_+
 \ge
 \left(s-1-qB_{d-1}\right)_+
 =(s-B_d)_+,
\]
because $B_d=1+qB_{d-1}$.  Thus, for $d\ge1$, the $d$-step survival
probability is at least $(s-B_d)_+/(s-1)$ when $s\ge2$.  Starting from
$L_q\ge2B_r$ gives probability greater than $1/2$, and among the $r$
recorded colours one occurs at least $K-1$ times.

\begin{corollary}[Multicolour size-biased Ramsey search]
\label{cor:multicolour}
For $q,K\ge2$, every $q$-edge-coloured complete graph on at least $L_q$
vertices admits a bounded-error quantum algorithm that outputs a
monochromatic $K$-clique using
\[
 O\!\left(r^2\sqrt{L_q}\log(2r)\log\frac1\eta\right)
\]
colour-oracle queries in the worst case, where $r$, $B_r$, and $L_q$ are as
defined above.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Executable audit}
\label{app:reproducibility}

The standard-library script
\path{experiments/quantum_ramsey/implicit_majority_audit.py} is invoked from
the repository root by
\begin{verbatim}
python3 \
  experiments/quantum_ramsey/implicit_majority_audit.py \
  --self-check --max-k 12 --simulate-k 3 \
  --trials 1000 --seed 20260831
\end{verbatim}
It verifies the dyadic error schedule, prefix-product inequality, and variable
recurrence with exact rational arithmetic through $K=64$.  It also compares
the size-biased survival lemma with an exact dynamic program over all split
trees through nine levels, emits the Jain-et-al./Liu--Zhandry exponent
translation, checks conditional marked-item uniformity by an explicit
16-dimensional Grover state-vector calculation, and runs deterministic-seed
diagnostics on complete, empty, parity, fixed random, and independently
resampled random graphs.

The end-to-end diagnostic replaces quantum search by an ideal uniform
marked-item sampler; only the isolated capped-search primitive receives the
small state-vector check.  These checks test the combinatorial invariant,
error schedule, and sampler symmetry.  They are not a simulation of the full
algorithm, a hardware experiment, or an empirical proof of quantum speedup.

The isolated 16-state calculation mixed Grover iteration counts from zero
through four.  Across marked-set sizes $1,2,3,4,8,15$, its block-success
probability ranged from $0.4026746749$ to $0.5973253251$, and the largest
conditional deviation from the uniform distribution on marked items was
zero to machine precision.  For the exhaustive split-tree checks, the
nine-level binary instance had exact worst survival $0.5086135566$ versus
the proved lower bound $513/1024$, and the four-level ternary instance had
exact worst survival $0.7155732457$ versus $88/127$.  Zero observed failures
in the table are a regression result, not an estimate that the failure
probability is zero.

For the hypergraph open problem in Section~7, quantum walks already give
sublinear-query algorithms for fixed sub-hypergraph containment
\cite{LeGallNishimuraTani2016}.  As a separate, speculative pointer,
higher-order symmetry representations have been useful in a different
hypergraph learning setting \cite{ZhangXinDey2024}.  Whether such structure can
be exposed with sublinear coherent queries in a Ramsey total-search problem is
open.
