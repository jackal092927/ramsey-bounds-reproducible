## Hyperbolic navigation response

Source: [HYPERBOLIC_INITIAL.md](responses/HYPERBOLIC_INITIAL.md).

The landmark algebra and fixed-dimensional model objection agree with the existing local proof. The global version requires access to nonlocal anchors; a local-only routing model instead pays for local anchors. Maximum-volume coefficients do not imply a well-conditioned Gram matrix: the two-anchor example at separation $\varepsilon$ has condition number asymptotic to $4/\varepsilon^2$.

The optimized progress-shadow formula is algebraically valid. If $d(p,q)=R$ and a candidate edge has free length $\ell$, progress $\delta\in(0,R)$ requires

$$\cos\theta\ge\frac{\cosh R\cosh\ell-\cosh(R-\delta)}{\sinh R\sinh\ell}.$$

Differentiating in $\ell$ makes the numerator of the derivative proportional to $\cosh(R-\delta)\cosh\ell-\cosh R$. Hence the unique optimum has $\cosh\ell_*=\cosh R/\cosh(R-\delta)$ and $\sin\alpha_*=\sinh(R-\delta)/\sinh R$. For fixed $\delta$, the far-field optimal angular cap stops shrinking with $R$.

This is a useful correction to an unrestricted use of the equal-length proxy. It does **not** refute the earlier formula under its explicit condition $\ell=R$, guarantee that a fixed graph contains the optimal edge, or prove impossibility for every quantum routing algorithm. Pro's $\Theta(dR)$ optimized proxy and broad stopping recommendation must retain those restrictions. Its spherical-shell reduction preserves exact score ordering; multiplicative-approximation parameters still need matching when used for ANN reductions. No new large navigation experiment is justified by the response.
