## Targeted novelty-collision pass, September 3

The new [hostile novelty audit](NOVELTY_COLLISION_AUDIT_2026-09-03.md) reopened the six ledger sources at the relevant theorem, lemma, definition and proof locations. It is a targeted pass rather than an exhaustive priority search.

- Crichigno--Kohler already makes the exact satisfying-state/hole correspondence parsimonious and obtains counting hardness. It does not supply the promised whole-kernel gap. Exact multiplicity cannot be claimed as new.
- King--Kohler's stated many-gadget theorem separates a nonempty-kernel YES case from a positive-definite NO case, but its proof already begins with arbitrary geometric chains and controls the logical and bulk sectors before applying \(H\succeq gI\). The degenerate-kernel extension is not stated there, yet it may be a short theorem-level corollary. This is the strongest novelty collision.
- Gyurik et al. already states a whole-final-kernel gap for its guided construction. Its displayed proof appears to apply a simulated-register lemma to an arbitrary geometric vector; this is an apparent domain mismatch, not a certified flaw or counterexample. The present all-chain derivation may be best framed as an independent repair/generalization.
- Lowe et al. already defines the true normalized ratio and explicitly asks for multiplicity, gaps and filtered compatibility. The present route bypasses literal compatible harmonic isometries only for nested term sets whose homology groups are naturally the quotients \(V/W_A\).
- Hayakawa's symmetric/asymmetric blow-up theorem plus common labeled multiplicities appears sufficient for endpoint kernels, induced rank and whole-gap scaling. The common-copy filtration argument is likely a direct corollary, not a main novelty claim.

No mathematical counterexample was found under the local theorem's explicit finite-certificate and independent-interior hypotheses. The current value rating is **MEDIUM, conditional**. It falls to LOW as a standalone theory result if the King--Kohler inequalities and Gyurik et al.'s existing argument already supply the same arbitrary-chain closure; it can rise only if the transfer theorem proves a reusable statement beyond those papers or the final result becomes unweighted with a more intrinsic denominator.
