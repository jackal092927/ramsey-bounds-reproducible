\section{Exact histories, a fixed clique palette, and hardness}
\label{sec:circuit}

We now instantiate the logical projector sums used in \cref{thm:transfer}. The source is an exact real-gate perfect-completeness computation rather than an arbitrary trace-threshold computation.

\subsection{A rational history for \texorpdfstring{\(G_2\)}{G2}}

Let
\[
G_2=\{X,\CX,\CCX,H\otimes H\}.
\]
For each \(H\otimes H\) transition, write \(K=\sqrt2H\). On a unary clock bit \(c\) and one work bit define the normalized forbidden vectors
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_0&=(\ket{00}+\ket{01}-\ket{10})/\sqrt3,&
u_1&=(\ket{00}-\ket{01}-\ket{11})/\sqrt3,\\
d_0&=(\ket{00}-\ket{10}-\ket{11})/\sqrt3,&
d_1&=(\ket{01}-\ket{10}+\ket{11})/\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two vectors in each pair are orthonormal. The corresponding projector sums enforce respectively
\[
f_{t+1}=\sqrt2Hf_t,
\qquad
f_{t+2}=Hf_{t+1}/\sqrt2.
\]
Apply the gain edge to the first work bit and the consecutive loss edge to the second. Their composite action is exactly \(H\otimes H\), and every rank-one projector has rational matrix entries. This gain/loss construction and its underlying three-term states are credited to Rudolph~\cite{rudolph2026universal}.

Transitions for \(X,\CX\), and \(\CCX\) use the usual two-time vectors
\[
(\ket{0,z}-\ket{1,Uz})/\sqrt2.
\]
For a fixed computational-basis string \(z\), such a vector factors into basis guards and either a one-qubit difference or a two-qubit difference state. Clock, input, and output penalties are basis projectors. Consequently every local term belongs to a fixed finite collection of rank-one real projectors with total support locality at most six.

The clique implementation uses only explicit members of this collection. The base palette consists of a basis cone, one one-qubit difference gadget, the two two-qubit difference states related by a register relabeling, and four Hadamard three-term states related by exact petal relabelings. Computational-basis tensor factors are imposed by a selected-cycle guard. For each resulting gadget, exact integer filling, the zero-weight kernel, and the projected private differential admit finite certificates of the form in \cref{ass:local-certificate}. This avoids any appeal to a general construction for arbitrary integer states.

\subsection{The two history kernels and their gaps}

Let \(L\) be the number of legal clock positions after splitting each Hadamard-pair transition. Put \(s_t=\sqrt2\) immediately after a gain edge and \(s_t=1\) at every other legal position, and let \(V_t\) be the unitary prefix after removing these scalar factors. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:history-isometry}
\cV v=Z^{-1/2}\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}s_t\ket tV_tv,
\qquad
Z=\sum_ts_t^2,
\qquad L\le Z\le2L.
\end{equation}
For the valid clean-input space \(C\), \(\cV:C\to\cK_{\rm in}\) is an isometry onto the complete zero space of the initial clock, propagation, and input Hamiltonian. Every zero-energy legal state is constant in prefix-rotated coordinates, and the input anchor forces that constant vector into \(C\); illegal clocks receive a positive clock penalty. Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:initial-kernel-gap}
\dim\cK_{\rm in}=D:=\dim C,
\qquad
g_{\rm in}\ge\frac1{8L^2}.
\end{equation}
The gap follows from a weighted path Poincar\'e inequality with vertex weights \(s_t^2\in[1,2]\), applied separately to the clean mean-zero sector and the dirty anchored sector.

Let \(M\) be the compressed acceptance operator from~\cref{eq:acceptance-operator}, and let \(S=\ker(I-M)\). Add the final rejection projector. Its compression to the initial history space is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:output-compression}
\cV^*H_{\rm rej}\cV=(I-M)/Z.
\end{equation}
Therefore positivity gives
\[
\ker H_{\rm out}=\cV S.
\]
Assume the off-perfect promise \(M|_{S^\perp}\preceq I/3\). \Cref{eq:output-compression} gives an angle penalty at least \(1/(3L)\) on \(\cK_{\rm in}\ominus\cV S\). The sharp two-positive-operator bound then yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:output-gap}
g_{\rm out}
\ge\frac1{3L(8L^2+1)}
\ge\frac1{27L^3}.
\end{equation}
This argument also covers \(S=0\), in which case~\cref{eq:output-gap} is a lower bound on the entire spectrum.

Combining~\cref{thm:transfer,thm:quotient,eq:initial-kernel-gap,eq:output-gap} gives weighted clique complexes \(\Xin\subseteq\Xout\) with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:generic-circuit-transfer}
\beta_d(\Xin)=D,
\qquad
\beta_d(\Xout)
=\beta_d(\Xin\to\Xout)
=\dim S,
\end{equation}
and inverse-polynomial gaps above both complete geometric kernels.

\subsection{An eight-label perfect-space source}

Let \(Q_x\) be a uniform exact \(G_2\) circuit on a fixed all-zero input, with one computational-basis output bit and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bqp1-promise}
x\in L\Longrightarrow p_x=1,
\qquad
x\notin L\Longrightarrow p_x\le\frac13.
\end{equation}
Add a maximally mixed label \(z\in\{0,1\}^3\), run \(Q_x\) unconditionally, and accept according to the reversible Boolean predicate
\[
A(z,q)=[z=000]\ \lor\
\bigl(q\land[z\in\{001,010,011,100,101\}]\bigr).
\]
The predicate uses only \(X,\CX,\CCX\), with scratch uncomputed before the final measurement. It does not control \(Q_x\), so it introduces no controlled Hadamard. Because the label is preserved, the acceptance operator on the mixed label space is exactly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eight-label}
M_x=\operatorname{diag}(1,p_x,p_x,p_x,p_x,p_x,0,0).
\end{equation}
In a YES instance, six diagonal entries equal one. In a NO instance, only the unconditional block equals one and every other eigenvalue is at most \(1/3\). Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eight-fractions}
\frac{\dim\ker(I-M_x)}8
=\begin{cases}
3/4,&x\in L,\\
1/8,&x\notin L.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-eight weighted hardness]
\label{thm:weighted-hardness}
There is a polynomial reduction from exact \(\BQPone^{G_2}\) verification to gapped true normalized persistence on nested weighted clique complexes. Every output satisfies
\[
\beta_d(\Xin)=8,
\]
and the persistent rank is six in YES instances and one in NO instances. Both endpoint degree-\(d\) Laplacians have inverse-polynomial gaps above their complete kernels. Hence additive approximation of~\cref{eq:true-ratio} with error less than \(5/16\) is \(\BQPone^{G_2}\)-hard; in particular, error \(1/24\) and threshold \(7/16\) suffice.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply~\cref{eq:generic-circuit-transfer} to~\cref{eq:eight-label}. The denominator is eight and~\cref{eq:eight-fractions} gives the two target values. Their distance is \(5/8\), so any additive error below half that distance separates them. The logical gaps~\cref{eq:initial-kernel-gap,eq:output-gap} and the polynomial number of history terms make~\cref{eq:weighted-gap} inverse polynomial in the circuit size.
\end{proof}

Rudolph proves exact simulation into \(G_2\) for each fixed finite gate family over the covered power-of-two cyclotomic fields~\cite{rudolph2026universal}. Composing that simulation with \cref{thm:weighted-hardness} gives hardness for each such gate family separately. Approximate universal compilation is insufficient because it need not preserve an eigenvalue-one space exactly.

The denominator in \cref{thm:weighted-hardness} is intentionally constant. Adding \(m\) ignored mixed bits tensors~\cref{eq:eight-label} with \(I_{2^m}\), multiplying both multiplicities by \(2^m\) and leaving the ratio unchanged. That closure is exact but padding-generated. We do not use it to suggest that the computation acts intrinsically on exponentially many distinct initial classes.

For comparison, an arbitrary normalized trace gap does not force a perfect-space fraction gap. If \(M|_{S^\perp}\preceq rI\), \(f=\dim S/D\), and \(p=\operatorname{Tr}(M)/D\), then
\[
f\le p\le r+(1-r)f.
\]
The operators \(I_{qD}\oplus0\) and \(I_{qD}\oplus\alpha I\) can have the same \(f=q\) while satisfying opposite trace promises. Consequently \cref{thm:weighted-hardness} uses perfect completeness explicitly and does not imply unrestricted \(\SDQCone\) hardness.

Finally, a circuit containing individual Hadamards can be represented using \(G_2\) by adding one maximally mixed, unmeasured spectator \(a\) and replacing each \(H_j\) by \(H_j\otimes H_a\). If the original circuit contains \(h\) Hadamards, then
\[
U'=U\otimes H_a^h,
\qquad
J'=J\otimes I_a,
\qquad
P_{\rm acc}'=P_{\rm acc}\otimes I_a,
\]
and, for either parity of \(h\),
\[
M'=(J')^*(U')^*P_{\rm acc}'U'J'=M\otimes I_a.
\]
This exact spectator identity preserves all fractions and off-perfect gaps. It establishes no complex-phase extension.
