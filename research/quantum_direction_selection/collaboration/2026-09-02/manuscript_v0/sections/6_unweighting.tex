\section{A natural unweighted corollary}
\label{sec:unweighting}

The weighted construction uses only weights \(1\) and a dyadic
inverse-polynomial \(\lambda\).  Hayakawa's common-copy construction replaces
each vertex \(v\) by a clique of \(f_v\) labeled copies and each base edge by
all cross-block edges~\cite{hayakawa2026unweighted}.  Choose one common
\(F=\lambda^{-2}\) and
\[
f_v=Fw(v)^2.
\]
Register vertices then have \(F\) copies and private vertices have one.

In degree \(k\), let \(U_{i,k}\) map a normalized base simplex to the
normalized average of its one-copy-per-block lifts.  Lemma~4.2 and
Theorem~4.3 of Hayakawa~\cite{hayakawa2026unweighted} give the symmetric
identity and asymmetric gap over real chains; scalar extension gives the same
statements over \(\mathbb C\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unweight-operator}
\widehat\Delta_{i,k}U_{i,k}=FU_{i,k}\Delta_{i,k},
\qquad
\widehat\Delta_{i,k}|_{\ran(U_{i,k})^\perp}\succeq
\min_v f_v\,I.
\end{equation}
Use the same labeled copy block for every shared vertex at both filtration
levels.  With consistent orientations, simplicial inclusion then satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unweight-natural}
\widehat J_{ij}U_{i,d}=U_{j,d}J_{ij}.
\end{equation}
There is no extra normalization factor because an inclusion preserves the
simplex degree, weights, labels, and copy set.

\begin{corollary}[Unweighted true normalized persistence]
\label{cor:unweighted-hardness}
The reduction in \cref{thm:weighted-hardness} maps in polynomial time to
nested unweighted clique complexes with the same endpoint Betti numbers,
persistent rank, and normalized values \(3/4\) and \(1/8\).  Both unweighted
endpoint Laplacians retain inverse-polynomial gaps above their complete
kernels.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(P_i\) and \(\widehat P_i\) be the weighted and unweighted harmonic
projectors.  The asymmetric bound in \cref{eq:unweight-operator} excludes
asymmetric harmonic vectors, while the symmetric identity identifies the
remaining zero modes.  Hence
\[
\ker\widehat\Delta_{i,d}=U_{i,d}\ker\Delta_{i,d},
\qquad
\widehat P_i=U_{i,d}P_iU_{i,d}^*.
\]
Combining this projector identity with \cref{eq:unweight-natural} gives
\[
\widehat P_j\widehat J_{ij}U_{i,d}
=U_{j,d}P_jJ_{ij}.
\]
Thus the Hodge realization of every inclusion-induced homology map is
unitarily equivalent to its weighted counterpart.  Endpoint nullities and
induced ranks are therefore preserved.  If \(E_i\) lower-bounds the positive
weighted spectrum, then
\[
\gamma_+(\widehat\Delta_{i,d})
\ge\min\{FE_i,\min_v f_v\}=\min\{FE_i,1\}.
\]
By \cref{eq:weighted-gap}, \(FE_i=
\Omega(\eta^{26}g_i/t_{\max}^{24})\).  Since \(F\) is polynomial, so is the
blown-up graph.  More explicitly, if the weighted graph has
\(O(n+t_{\max})\) vertices and \(O(n^2+t_{\max}n)\) edges, a safe explicit
bound after blow-up is \(O(F(n+t_{\max}))\) vertices and
\(O(F^2(n^2+t_{\max}n))\) edges; labels and edges are generated by nested
loops in polynomial time.  Future private vertices may be present initially as
isolated vertices; the target degree is positive, so they add no target-degree
chain.
\end{proof}

The operator decomposition is Hayakawa's; the filtration-specific step is
the commuting identity \cref{eq:unweight-natural}.  We do not count the
unweighting mechanism as a separate contribution.
