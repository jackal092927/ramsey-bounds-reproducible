\section{Limitations and open problems}
\label{sec:limitations}

The construction has three substantive limits.  Its exact palette is finite
and real: arbitrary integer states and complex phases are outside the
theorem, so every hardness statement is relative to the gate set \(G_2\)
(extended to cyclotomic gate sets only for \(\BQPone\), through Rudolph's
simulation).  Trace-form \(\SDQCone\)-hardness is proved only for threshold
pairs with \(b<(3a-1)/2\), and \cref{app:source-boundaries} shows that this
is the limit of any reduction whose target sees only the perfectly accepted
subspace.  Finally, the gap is a complexity promise rather than a practical
estimate: at depth five it scales conservatively as
\(\Omega(\eta^{12}g/t^{10})\) with \(g=\Omega(L^{-3})\), and the common-copy
blow-up multiplies the vertex count by \(F=\lambda^{-2}\).

Each limit suggests a question.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does \(\SF^{G}\)-hardness extend to every finite gate set
\(G\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^k})\)?  Rudolph's simulation accepts when its
postselection fails; on an \(\SF\) verifier this keeps \(S_x\) perfectly
accepted and, if the failure probability is input-independent and
exponentially small, keeps the soundness on \(S_x^\perp\) at
\(1/3+2^{-\poly}\).  Verifying these two properties would remove most of the
gate dependence.
\item Is there an exact transformation of an \(\SDQCone\) verifier whose
perfect fraction encodes the acceptance probability of the maximally mixed
state?  If not, the fraction form is the right definition of the class, and
Conjecture~1 of Lowe et al.\ should be read in that form.
\item Can the depth be reduced?  A palette whose fillings use at most two
private vertices per simplex would give \(\kappa=4\) and
\(E=\Omega(\eta^6g/t^4)\).
\item Lowe et al.\ ask for a general kernel-preserving universality theorem
for weighted clique Laplacians.  \Cref{thm:transfer} is such a theorem for
palettes with finite certificates.  Extending the certificate model to
integer-state gadgets~\cite{king2026gapped} or to complex phases would answer
their question for a much wider class of Hamiltonians.
\item Do the hard instances also have inverse-polynomial gaps of the
persistent Laplacian~\cite{memoli2022persistent}?  This is not needed for
\cref{thm:advantage}, but it would connect the instances to the persistent
Betti number algorithms of~\cite{hayakawa2022quantum,mcardle2026streamlined}.
\end{enumerate}

Within its limits, the paper establishes a reusable criterion: a new clique
reduction obtains exact kernel multiplicity, an inverse-polynomial gap above
the whole kernel, harmonic spaces that track the logical kernels, and natural
persistent ranks by certifying, for a finite palette, local zero-weight
kernels, private coercivity, exact filling, and interfaces, with no
growing-dimensional perturbation argument.
